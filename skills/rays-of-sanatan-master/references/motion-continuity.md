# Scene-first motion and continuity

Original Rays adaptation informed by Beyondsumi's scene-first, continuity,
representative-shot and review practices. Its ink aesthetic and drawing-clock
defaults are not part of this photorealistic channel style.

## Whole shot before layers

Plan camera, ground plane, scale, light, props and occlusion in one coherent
master. Derive coordinated layers from that scene or controlled assets; do
not assemble unrelated limbs/objects and expect consistent perspective.
A crop is not a newly generated viewpoint.

Prove the hardest action first: heavy stone motion, grip, pouring, walking or
dialogue. Inspect normal-speed motion and adjacent frames before spending on
a batch. Reuse valid work for revisions; no episode rebuild for a typo.

## Continuity ledger

Use one frame ledger for contacts, cuts, voice cues and SFX. For every action
record actor/prop ID, initial position/support, grip/contact, route/screen
direction, pose/occlusion, consequence and next-shot state. At cut, can the
object and person's knowledge/gesture plausibly continue?

Contact SFX follow actual visible contact, not arbitrary scene midpoints.
Keep release/tails and voice ducking at measured anchors. A narrative sound
stop needs an audible lead-in; don't imply a sound played when it didn't.

## Actual action

Breathing/light/dust/camera motion enrich a scene but cannot replace walking,
grip changes, load transfer or consequence. Labels and high fps are not new
coherent artwork. If using drawings, count original art, derived states,
reused cycles and exposures separately. Don't inherit Sumi's 12/20-state
targets as universal quality metrics for realistic films.

Check feet on ground, real grip, plausible rope/load, no stone penetration,
coherent shadow and scale. Generated motion needs review; don't falsely call
it a physical simulation. Preserve stillness when the emotion needs it.

## Dialogue

Narrator speech is off-screen unless a character explicitly speaks. Don't
animate a deity/person's mouth to narrator prose. Dialogue needs voice casting,
actual timing and supported lip sync. If tools are absent, use supported
staging and disclose limits; don't promise speech animation from stills.
